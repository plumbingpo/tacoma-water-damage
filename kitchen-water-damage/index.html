Kitchen Water Damage in Tacoma

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. Our Tacoma crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

What It Looks Like

  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill
  • Sticking cabinet doors, or doors whose edges no longer match up
  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
  • Wood or laminate flooring that cups or lifts in front of the dishwasher
  • A damp, stale smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
  • Toe kicks that have swollen, darkened, or pulled away from the cabinet base
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Drips at the Sink Drain

A loose drain fitting or disposal seal leaks only while the sink is draining, so it is easy to miss. Food waste rides along in that water, so the sink cabinet turns smelly and moldy quickly.

The Ice Maker Line

An ice maker is fed by a thin tube that may lose a few drops each minute. Behind a fridge that never moves, that is enough to rot flooring and the wall behind.

The Dishwasher Cavity

The subfloor and neighboring cabinet sides get wet when the drain or supply connection drips slowly. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Stop the Source

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Map the Moisture

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry in Place

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first hour

Water Runs Under

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Begin to Swell

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Day 3 to 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.
