Mitigation means one thing: keeping the damage from getting any bigger. Repair is a different job. Mitigation covers the steps taken ahead of repairs so undamaged materials stay that way. Our Tacoma crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. Good mitigation makes the later repair smaller, and now and then makes it unnecessary.

People often use mitigation and restoration to mean the same thing. Each is its own phase of the job. During mitigation, the urgent part, the loss is halted, its spread is limited and the home is made stable. Once the structure tests dry, restoration takes over and covers repairing or rebuilding finishes, drywall and floors that were damaged. Insurance companies also bill the two as separate line items, which makes it useful to know your current phase.

On a standard mitigation call we work through four items. First is source control, which keeps more water from coming in. Extraction, so standing water is gone. Three: contain the area with plastic sheeting and managed airflow, so clean rooms stay free of moisture and contaminants. Fourth is stabilization: unsalvageable materials come out, surfaces receive antimicrobial treatment, and dehumidifiers and air movers are placed. Furniture and personal items are protected too, either relocated or raised on blocks.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Mitigation

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Leave the heat on during winter trips so the plumbing cannot freeze.
  • Ground next to the foundation should fall away from it, which keeps water from collecting there.
  • Keep gutters clear and downspouts pointed away from the foundation of the house.
  • Install an automatic shutoff valve that closes the main when a leak is detected.
  • Have your water heater inspected yearly and replace it before the tank fails.
  • A main shutoff that will not turn is no help, so locate and test yours.

How to Tell You Need Water Damage Mitigation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A roof or window opening is letting rain into the building.
  • An adjuster or agent said you must reasonably limit additional damage.
  • Carpet squishes underfoot, or the pad beneath it is saturated.
  • Water is no longer flowing, though it has left wet floors, cabinets or walls behind.
  • Along the floor, water is travelling out of one room and into another.
  • Plumbing repairs are done, but no one has addressed what got wet.
  • Following a water loss, indoor air stays damp and condensation forms on the glass.

Water Damage Mitigation, Step by Step

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Stop the Water Source

Depending on the cause, our crew closes the supply, caps a failed line, or covers the entry point with tarps and boards. If a plumber or roofer is needed for the permanent fix, we coordinate the timing.

2

Extract the Water

Pumps and extractors take out the standing water, and we inspect the carpet and pad. Fast removal of bulk water limits damage more than drying equipment could by itself.

3

Set Up Containment

Wet rooms are closed off from dry ones with barriers of plastic sheeting and controlled airflow. That way dust, humidity and contaminants do not move through the home during the work.

4

Stabilize and Dry

We remove what cannot be saved, treat surfaces with antimicrobial, and place dehumidifiers and air movers. Each visit includes logged moisture readings, and the structure is usually ready for repair work within 3 to 5 days.

Why Professional Water Damage Mitigation Matters

Mitigation is a series of judgment calls made quickly. What drywall stays and dries where it is, and what must be removed? Is the water Category 1, 2 or 3, and has it changed since the leak began? Is removing the cabinet toe kick necessary? Each decision rests on meter readings and our training, never on whether something seems dry to the eye or hand.

Your insurance claim benefits from proper mitigation too. Owners are required by most policies to take reasonable steps against further damage, and the insurer will ask for evidence. We photograph conditions on arrival, record moisture readings daily and keep a list of anything removed. Together those records show the water's effect and our response to it. The repair contractor gets a clear place to start as well, without hidden moisture waiting behind fresh drywall.
