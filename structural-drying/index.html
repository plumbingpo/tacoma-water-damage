No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

The structural drying technicians at Tacoma Restoration Co are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

How to Tell You Need Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Condensation forming on windows in the area that was wet
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Wood flooring with boards that cup, crown or pull apart
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, peels or blisters after a leak
  • Drywall that is soft, swollen or shows moisture staining
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams

Why Structural Drying Is Not a DIY Job

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
  • Each unit is positioned according to the moisture map, so ask before shifting anything
  • Every hour of run time matters, so resist switching the machines off for some quiet
  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome
  • Until a technician removes the equipment, keep it running day and night without breaks
  • Shut the doors and windows of any room under drying, which keeps dehumidification working well
